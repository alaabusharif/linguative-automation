# Linguative — Brand Guide

Authoritative visual identity and design rules for every marketing/design agent and routine in this repo. Supplied by Ala (2026-09-23). Treat every rule here as mandatory unless Ala explicitly overrides it for a specific task.

## Brand positioning

Linguative is a premium language, interpretation, conference technology, audiovisual, and event-services company. The visual identity should feel:
international, premium, intelligent, human, precise, technologically capable, calm and confident.

## Logo — strict rule

There are **two approved and locked** logo files, both stored in this repo at `marketing/brand/logos/`:

1. **`logo-navy-gold-light-bg.png`** (navy + gold) — use on white, ivory, cream, pale grey, and other **light** backgrounds.
2. **`logo-white-gold-dark-bg.png`** (white + gold) — use on navy, charcoal, black, dark photography, and other **dark** backgrounds.

These supplied files are authoritative. **Never**: redraw the logo; re-type the word "LINGUATIVE"; reconstruct it using fonts or vector shapes; regenerate it with AI; change its proportions; modify the gold swoosh; change the typography; add symbols/icons/globes/microphones/people or other elements to it; use previous/experimental logo concepts; invent a third variation. Always place the supplied artwork as an asset, with generous clear space around it, never stretched.

Official tagline, always kept exactly as written: **COMMUNICATION BEYOND LANGUAGE.**

## Typography

- **Futura PT** — headings, navigation, labels
- **Acumin Pro** — body copy, captions, proposals, technical content
- **Cormorant Garamond Semibold Italic** — occasional editorial/campaign accents only

Fallback stack: Montserrat, Source Sans 3, Georgia. Do not use decorative fonts merely to make a design look luxurious.

## Design language

Aim for premium editorial design, not generic corporate templates.

**Use:** generous negative space; strong asymmetrical compositions; large, confident typography; one dominant visual idea per page/slide; thin champagne-gold rules and dividers; carefully controlled navy fields; warm ivory backgrounds; elegant photographic crops; golden-ratio-inspired proportions and hierarchy where practical; strong alignment and disciplined grids.

**Avoid:** busy layouts; excessive gradients; generic speech bubbles; random flags; repeated arcs and swooshes (beyond the one in the logo itself); cheap "luxury" effects; overuse of icons; generic stock-photo compositions; AI-looking conference technology.

## Photography

Preferred subjects: international conferences; interpreter booths; professional interpreters; conference microphones and discussion systems; Bosch DICENTIS equipment; language distribution equipment; high-level meetings; training workshops; event stages and LED screens; professional video production; document translation/editing environments; hybrid meeting technology.

Photography should feel realistic, premium, and documentary rather than staged. Technical equipment must remain credible — do not invent controls, ports, microphone designs, or hybrid devices. When a real product photo is supplied, use that exact photo rather than generating an imitation; background removal and subtle perspective/crop adjustments are fine, but don't materially alter the equipment.

## Content tone

Clear, concise, professional, international, human, confident without exaggeration. Avoid generic AI marketing language and unnecessary superlatives.

## PowerPoint / presentation rules

Editability is mandatory. All of the following must be native editable PowerPoint elements: titles, body text, captions, labels, lines, dividers, boxes, cards, background color fields, icons where practical, decorative geometric elements. Do **not** build a whole slide as one flattened image pasted into PowerPoint. Photographs may stay as raster images, but slide structure and typography must remain editable.

When recreating a slide from a visual reference: match the approved composition closely; don't redesign it unless asked; if asked to change one item, change only that item and preserve the rest; don't substitute a different design just because it's easier to generate.

## Quality control before delivery

Before exporting any design, verify: correct locked logo version used; logo not recreated/modified; brand colors correct (exact hex values above, not approximations); typography follows the brand system; layout feels premium and uncluttered; technical equipment realistic; no unnecessary design elements introduced; spelling/grammar correct; for PowerPoint, everything that can reasonably be editable is editable; the delivered design follows the exact requested change rather than redesigning unrelated elements.

**Most important rule:** preserve approved designs. When Ala requests a small revision to an existing design, do not reinterpret or rebuild the whole composition — make the requested change only.
