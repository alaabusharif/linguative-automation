## Why this article

The live Insights inventory contains guidance on simultaneous versus consecutive interpretation, equipment briefs, hybrid events, AV, procurement and interpreter preparation. It does not give organisers a method for sizing an interpretation team across language directions, parallel rooms, duration and relay.

An internal 20 October brief had proposed the buyer question “How many interpreters does a full-day conference need?” That brief used a useful rule of thumb but was too fixed for an article. This draft replaces it with an agenda-based method and makes the dependencies explicit.

The article does not assert search volume or a stable ranking. It addresses a plausible procurement question and supports Linguative’s one-accountable-team positioning across interpretation, technology and event delivery.

## SEO brief

- **Buyer intent:** event organisers, institutions and procurement teams trying to scope or compare interpretation staffing.
- **Primary question:** How many interpreters does a conference need?
- **Arabic question:** كم مترجمًا فوريًا يحتاج المؤتمر؟
- **Distinct purpose:** calculate staffing from concurrent interpreted rooms, language directions, mode, duration and relay. This is different from the existing mode-comparison and equipment-brief articles.
- **On-page choices:** the exact buyer question is the title; the opening gives a qualified answer; headings cover the decision variables; no location doorway page is created.
- **Internal links:** simultaneous interpretation, consecutive interpretation, conference technology/AV and event/conference management.
- **CTA:** send the agenda for a staffing plan. No “top rated,” “best,” ranking promise or fabricated volume claim.

## Public-source evidence

1. **AIIC Asia-Pacific, “FAQ about interpreting”**  
   https://aiic.asia/faq/  
   Supports: there is no single staffing number; languages, mode, meeting length and breakouts matter; simultaneous conference work commonly needs 2–3 interpreters per active language and is team work.

2. **European Commission Directorate-General for Interpretation, “Standards for interpreting facilities”**  
   https://commission.europa.eu/about/departments-and-executive-agencies/interpretation/standards-interpreting-facilities_en  
   Supports: simultaneous-interpreting work environments and equipment are governed by current technical standards; staffing and technical setup should not be planned as unrelated lines.

3. **United Nations DGACM, “Interpretation Service”**  
   https://www.un.org/dgacm/en/content/interpretation-service  
   Supports: language combinations, subject matter and delivery speed are material operational considerations in professional interpretation.

4. **Google Search Central, “AI features and your website”**  
   https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features  
   Used for GEO QA: people-first content and standard technical SEO remain the foundation; no special AI schema or guaranteed AI visibility is claimed.

## Claim boundaries

- “At least two” is presented as a common starting point for a continuously active simultaneous booth/output, not a universal quotation formula.
- No fixed shift interval, maximum working day or blanket “two per language pair” rule is published.
- Consecutive staffing is described as scope-dependent.
- The example is hypothetical, not an anonymised or implied client case.
- No client or equipment brand is named.
