## Objective
Head off the most common price objection before it reaches a quote ("why two interpreters?"). In a price-sensitive market, buyers who understand why simultaneous needs a team compare on quality, not on the cheapest single-interpreter bid. That protects margin and win rate.

## Audience
Budget holders and procurement staff drafting RFQs; first-time event organizers.

## Key message
Simultaneous interpretation is done in teams. For a full day, each language pair normally needs **two interpreters in the booth**, taking turns so accuracy holds from the opening session to the last one. Consecutive or short meetings may need fewer. More language pairs means more teams. "We'll tell you honestly what your agenda needs, no more."

## Slide outline (carousel)
1. "How many interpreters does your conference need?" / «كم مترجمًا فوريًا يحتاج مؤتمرك؟»
2. Why teams: simultaneous is intense work; interpreters rotate at regular intervals so quality doesn't drop late in the day.
3. Rule of thumb: full day, one language pair (e.g. Arabic-English) = a team of two. Each added pair = another team (e.g. adding French).
4. When fewer can work: short meetings, consecutive formats. Ask us.
5. CTA: "Send us your agenda and languages; we'll size the team and the setup." + logo
